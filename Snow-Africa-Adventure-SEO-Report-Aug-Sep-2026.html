What this means

The positive

Momentum is real but early. The keyword footprint grew from 512 (June) to 783 (August) — the site is being found for substantially more searches than three months ago. One keyword entered the top 3 for the first time in this tracking set, and six keywords that were not ranking at all have entered the top 100. The group-departures cluster is the standout: "kilimanjaro joining group departures" climbed from position 13 to position 2.

The honest caveat

Two more keywords fell out of the top 100 than entered it (45 not ranking, up from 43), and eight keywords dropped out entirely — including "tanzania camping safari", which was at position 17. Average position improved because the keywords that are ranking moved up, not because more keywords started ranking. Both things are true at once, and the second one needs attention.

Where the bookings actually are

Domain Authority is 12, against 33–47 for the competitors we share most keywords with. That gap does block the big informational terms. But it does not block the keywords that actually produce bookings: on "best kilimanjaro companies" a DA-14 site ranks second and a DA-5 site ranks seventh, ahead of Reddit and two DA-50 operators.

There is a cluster of roughly 1,100 searches a month around choosing a Kilimanjaro operator, where advertisers pay $15–20 per click and difficulty is low. You already have pages for it. They currently rank for nothing. This is the single clearest commercial opportunity on the site and is set out in Sections 06 and 07.

Two findings that outrank everything else

Google has never crawled 293 of your pages, including the commercial ones. The blog archive had no pagination, so 271 of 291 articles had no inbound link. Fixed this period — Section 08.

33 of 35 enquiries have never been actioned, among them roughly $15,000–22,000 of identified revenue still waiting for a reply. Section 09.

Work completed this period

14 releases across two working sessions (18 August and 3 September), totalling 80 files changed and 3,158 lines added. Every item below is a shipped, deployed change verified live on the production site.

Search engine optimisation 8 RELEASES

  • Page title overhaul. 72 page titles were being truncated by Google because they exceeded the ~60-character display limit. All were rewritten to fit — 30 in August (28 static pages plus 2 found on a deeper recrawl), then 42 more in September covering blog articles, safari packages, trekking routes and day trips.
  • Duplicate title tags eliminated. Pages competing against each other in search results for the same title were given distinct, specific titles.
  • Double-branding bug fixed. Ten titles rendered as "… | Snow Africa Adventure | Snow Africa Adventure", wasting display space in results. Corrected to a single brand mention.
  • 12 broken internal links repaired. Flagged by audit; all now resolve, so link equity flows correctly and visitors no longer hit dead ends.
  • Blog page refresh restored. Blog listing, article and category pages were serving stale cached versions; revalidation was fixed so new content appears promptly.

User experience & front-end 4 RELEASES

  • Layout shift eliminated on the group-departures page — content no longer jumps as the page loads. This is a ranking factor (Core Web Vitals) as well as a usability issue.
  • Broken certification logos fixed in the footer, which were rendering as grey boxes and undermining trust signals (KPAP, TATO and similar credentials).
  • Expired review widget removed. A lapsed Trustindex widget was loading on every page; removed, and the site favicon was refreshed.
  • Brand slogan added to the header, and the hero badge styling was cleaned up.

Content structure 2 RELEASES

  • Group-departures page restructured — content reordered and de-duplicated so the booking information reads in a logical sequence.
  • Rendering fault repaired that could break the departures page under certain navigation conditions.

Independently verified

After the work, an independent Ubersuggest crawl of 722 pages returned a health score of 99.61 / 100 with zero long titles, zero empty titles, zero missing H1 tags, zero broken links and zero 4xx errors. We separately checked all 703 URLs in the sitemap by hand and reached the same result.

Where the business actually stands

Snow Africa Adventure has the lowest Domain Authority in its own competitive set and by far the fewest backlinks — 1,127 against 8,694 for the next-smallest serious competitor and 270,139 for Altezza. This is not a content problem. The site now has 291 published articles and a near-perfect technical score; the pages are there and they are sound.

The obvious conclusion would be that authority alone decides everything. Checking the actual search results shows that is only half true — and the half that is false is the commercially important half.

Authority blocks some keywords — but not the ones that sell

We examined the live results page for three keyword types. On informational terms, authority dominates: for "best time to climb Kilimanjaro" the lowest-authority site in the top 17 is DA 36, Reddit holds position 2, and an AI Overview sits above everything.

On operator-selection terms the picture reverses completely. For "best kilimanjaro companies", a DA-14 site ranks second — ahead of Reddit (DA 92), Facebook (DA 96) and two DA-50 operators — and a DA-5 site ranks seventh. At DA 12, Snow Africa Adventure is squarely inside that range today, with no new links required.

Two conclusions that follow

Head terms remain out of reach. "Tanzania safaris" (12,100/month) and "climbing kilimanjaro" (6,600/month) are not realistically winnable in the next 6–12 months. We would rather say so than report against targets that cannot be met.

Position on the page matters more than the page itself. Across every results page we examined, position 2 drew 23–278 clicks while positions 6–15 drew between 0 and 4. Reaching page one is not the goal — reaching the top three is. This is why the group-departures result (position 2) is worth more than a dozen rankings in the thirties.

Why this is the priority

Advertisers pay roughly $20 a click for "kilimanjaro tour operator" because the person searching it is choosing who to hand $2,000–4,000 to. Google classifies the intent as Commercial. Difficulty is 17–24, which is low — and as Section 06 showed, sites at DA 5 and DA 14 already rank here.

You have three pages built for exactly this cluster — /best-kilimanjaro-tour-operators/ (4,175 words), /kilimanjaro-climbing-companies/ (3,243 words) and /best-tanzania-tour-operator/. They are substantial and internally linked. All three currently rank for nothing in the United States. The asset exists; the visibility does not.

What the sites that win are doing differently

Operators who rank for these terms do it with their homepage, titled as a company, not with a "best operators" article: "Home — Best Kilimanjaro Company" (DA 5, position 7), "The #1 Guide Company for Climbing Kilimanjaro" (position 8), "Climb Kilimanjaro with Local Experts | 98.8% Success" (DA 30, position 9). Each pairs the category word with a credibility claim.

The Snow Africa homepage is titled "Tanzania Safaris & Kilimanjaro Treks" — no "operator", no "company", no proof point. The phrase "locally owned Tanzania tour operator" appears only in the meta description, which carries no ranking weight. That is a low-effort, high-leverage fix.

Separately: the one site beating everyone at position 2 is an independent reviewer, not an operator. A tour operator publishing its own "best tour operators" list faces an inherent credibility problem for that query — which may explain why the existing pages have not taken hold. Being listed on independent review sites is likely to outperform writing our own.

What Search Console reports

293 pages sit under “Discovered — currently not indexed” with a last-crawl date of N/A. These are commercial pages: the Kilimanjaro tour-operator guide, African safaris, Big Five Tanzania, budget safaris, climbing from the USA. Inspecting the operator page directly returns “URL is not on Google” and, critically, “Referring page: None detected”.

Google found these pages in the sitemap, saw nothing on the site linking to them, judged them unimportant and never spent the crawl budget. The 4,175-word operator guide has never been fetched.

The cause: the blog archive had no pagination

The blog index listed only the 20 most recent posts and offered no way to reach the rest — no page 2, no “load more”. 271 of 291 published articles had no inbound link anywhere on the site. Years of content was invisible to Google, not because it was poor, but because nothing pointed at it.

Fixed during this period

The archive is now paginated across 15 pages, verified to expose 311 post links — every published article is reachable. Six commercial pages that had no inbound link at all, including one already earning 1,947 monthly impressions at position 8.6, were added to the site-wide footer. Indexing was requested directly in Search Console for the highest-value pages.

Recovery is not immediate. Google must re-crawl, follow the new links and reassess: expect 2–6 weeks, and some pages may still not be crawled at this domain authority.
